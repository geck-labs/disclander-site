Battles

Blows

Section titled “Blows”

Attacks come from four directions: left, right, overhead and thrust. Each has a windup, a swing and a recovery, and you can see which side a swing is coming from. Once let go, the blade takes 0.3 s to sweep its arc and lands on the first man it reaches: a side cut on a man to its side early and on one ahead late, an overhead and a thrust partway through. A block stops only an attack from the side it covers, and only from in front. Blocking during a windup feints into the block.

Parries

Section titled “Parries”

Two blows that cross parry each other: an overhead met by a left or right swing, a left or right swing met by an overhead, and a thrust beaten aside by a left or right swing. The same against the same (left into left) does not cross, and both men are hit. Crossing blows must reach their men within 0.15 s of each other, in the lists too. Then the blades ring, sparks fly and neither lands. The first to arrive is parried and thrown into a longer recovery (more against a heavier weapon or a stronger arm); the other wins the tempo and has 0.6 s to start a riposte, a windup half as long, which he must still aim. Pick a side that does not cross, or mistime it, and his blow lands in full, because you were swinging, not blocking.

Drags and accels

Section titled “Drags and accels”

While your blade is in its swing, turning moves it. On a side cut, turning against the swing drags it (it lands later, down to 0.6 of its pace, and a little softer) and turning with it speeds it (up to 1.4). On an overhead, looking up drags it and looking down brings it in. You turn no faster than a cap while swinging. Parries go by the moment the blades really meet, so a drag can bait a man into countering too early.

Speed adds harm:

Closing speed Harm
Standing 1x
14.4 m/s 2x
Faster Up to 3x
Pulling away Down to 0.25x

Every blow is a force: the blade’s weight at its swing’s speed, plus the striker’s travel. A cut from the left throws a man to the right, an overhead drives him down, and a thrust drives him back. A light blow makes him jolt, a harder one stumble him back, and one of 5 m/s or more knocks him off his feet. A rider holds his seat against half as much again, and past that is knocked out of the saddle. A braced spear takes a rider’s charge.

To cut a man down at the gallop, hold a left or right swing out to the side and let go a little before he comes up: the blade sweeps round and strikes the first man it reaches. In the saddle a side cut sweeps well down, through a man on foot’s chest.

Your blade leaves a pixel trail exactly where it went through the cut, thinning away over 0.35 s. A swing that lands turns it gold, so a miss shows how far short or wide it went.

Any horse over 9 m/s runs down hostile men on foot within 1.4 m: 20 harm at 10.8 m/s, about 37 at a full sprint. Spearmen brace instead, and a horseman galloping onto a braced spear is hurt as if trampled.

Roles

Section titled “Roles”

A man’s role is what his kit and seat make him (see Equipment).

Role HP Notes
Infantry (spearman) 100 Longest reach; braces; does 4.25x to horsemen
Ranged (archer) 80 Shoots within 40 m every 1.8 s, 90% point blank down to 40% at full range, 30 harm
Cavalry (horseman) 200 Hits harder the faster it closes; tramples men on foot
Ranged cavalry (mounted archer) Shoots from the saddle, out of the infantry’s reach

Infantry beats cavalry, cavalry beats ranged and ranged cavalry, ranged beats infantry and outshoots ranged cavalry, and ranged cavalry beats infantry, at equal numbers and equal coin. On the map, a role hits one it beats twice as hard and one that beats it half as hard. A battle fought out on the map counts each man’s gear, skills and attributes, so a well-armed, skilled army hits harder and lasts longer than its numbers say.

The field

Section titled “The field”

Far armies are banners on the map. When one comes within 300 m of the camera it turns into soldiers, and past 380 m it goes back to being a banner, with everything kept. In co-op every battle is resolved on the map.

Soldiers cross deep water by a bridge or ford within 500 m, and walls by an open gate. Hedges, walls and fences stop them, and they go round by the gates. Mud and snow slow men and horses, and rain and gusts make arrows fall short. Enemies and horses can shove men over the rim.

Stations and captains

Section titled “Stations and captains”

Every squad has a numbered station in the army, one squad to a station: vanguard, main body, left flank, right flank, reserve, rearguard and skirmishers, each 1, 2, 3 and so on. You set them on the Party screen, where you also mark the squad you ride with, your squadron, and pick a second in command (else the best at warfare outside your squadron). A new squad takes a station by its men: foot the main body, archers the skirmishers, horse the right flank and horse archers the left.

Station Where it stands Its job
Vanguard Ahead on the march, at the front of the field Closes and charges, and falls back on the body when pressed
Main body The centre Holds the line and charges foes who come near
Left and right flank Beside the body Guards its side; horse charge an enemy band locked in the fight
Reserve Behind the body Goes in on its horn, when our army wavers or theirs breaks
Rearguard Last on the march, by the train Goes for foes at the train
Skirmishers Out in front Shoot, and fall back behind the body as the enemy comes on

Each squad’s leader is its captain, and he acts on his own. He looks about him every 2 s, every half second at 100 warfare, and sets his squad’s place, facing, target, hold fire and shape: riders in a wedge, shooters in open order, foot in a line that closes into a block when enemy horse are near. His temper, cautious to bold, sets how near he lets the enemy come before he charges (20 to 45 m), how much he takes before he gives ground, and whether he chases a beaten foe. A captain with little warfare misjudges both, by up to a third. AI armies fight the same way, under captains at their stations.

Your squadron rides round you, riders in a wedge with you at its point. Your riding is the order: gallop at foes within 45 m ahead and they charge with you; otherwise they follow you.

Orders

Section titled “Orders”

B sends every squad to its battle station round you, facing your way, the men walking there from wherever they stand. The captains then wait for the Advance horn, so you can form up and stand off. Nothing pops up.

Hold Tab for the horn wheel. A horn call reaches every captain at once:

Call What it does
Advance The captains start their station jobs
Retire Every squad falls back in order
Rally Every squad gathers on its banner
Reserve, go Only the reserve goes in

Look at a squad’s banner and hold Z for its order wheel: point at an order and let go (right click shuts it). An order holds until Back to station, in the middle, drops it.

Order What it does
Charge Charge
Press forward Close on the enemy step by step
Pursue Chase a beaten enemy, or let him go
Hold Stand where it is
Rally Gather close on the banner
Withdraw Leave the fight in good order
Fall back Give ground facing the enemy
Follow me Come behind you
Loose Shooters: shoot at will, or hold fire

A captain within 30 m hears your shout and answers in his own time. Further off, a rider of your squadron gallops the order over, and it holds only once he gets there. If he is cut down on the way, it never arrives.

The number keys still pick the supply train: right drag then places it, lined up in as many files as the drag was wide.

Squad leaders and banners

Section titled “Squad leaders and banners”

Every squad has a leader, its captain, paid twice. His squad answers an order in 2.5 s, less 0.2 s a point of his warfare, 0.5 s at best. When he falls the squad wavers 3 s before the next best takes over, and the army loses 0.05 morale.

Every squad of two or more flies a banner with its device in the army’s colour. A banner drops where its bearer falls, and the nearest man within 25 m goes for it. The army loses 0.1 morale while it is down, gets 0.05 back when its own take it up, and loses 0.1 again if the enemy carries it off.

Formations

Section titled “Formations”

The battle formation lays the stations out: skirmishers in front, then the vanguard, the main body with the flanks beside it (1 nearest the body), then the reserve and the rearguard, numbered stations of a row side by side.

M takes up the marching formation and Ctrl+M keeps the current plan as it. The Squads tab keeps up to nine plans by name.

On the march the army is a column by station: vanguard, skirmishers, left flank, main body, right flank and reserve, then the train, and the rearguard last, covering it. You ride in your squadron’s place, not at the head, so make your squadron the vanguard to lead. The column strings out along the road and bends with it, as many abreast as the way allows: 4 on a road or open ground, 2 on a track, and in file through a gate, over a bridge or across a ford, where the men queue in column order. A hundred men four abreast is about 50 m of road, and a big army with its train a kilometre. Press B on the march and the vanguard is ready while the rearguard is still walking up. See Parties.

Armies with nobody commanding them form up by doctrine when an enemy comes within 400 m. They charge at 1.5 to one and run below 0.6 to one.

Morale

Section titled “Morale”

Armies start at 0.8 morale. Losses cost it: losing a share of the army costs 0.75 times that share. Hunger and unpaid wages cost it too. Below 0.3 an army breaks and runs, or below 0.45 once it has lost two squad leaders.

A routed army runs for the nearest friendly town or castle within 8 km, else its train, and rallies within 150 m of it. Elsewhere it rallies at 0.6 morale once no pursuer is within 1.2 km. A pursuer chases only by day, under 0.75 weary and within 3 km of its own train. After dark armies fight on only if one is set on the other.

Weariness

Section titled “Weariness”

Men and horses tire by Breath (seconds: sprints, swings, blocks), Rest (hours: the march and the fight) and their stomachs. Winded men swing slower and weaker and their blocks break. Low Rest and an empty stomach hurt only by leaving a man less breath. Away from you, an army’s men fight a map battle with the breath their rest and stomachs leave them, so spent men lose to as many fresh ones. All of it is on Fatigue.

Camps

Section titled “Camps”

H makes camp where you stand: tents, fires, horse lines, a wagon park and a sentry ring. It goes up in ten game minutes and comes down in a quarter hour. Riders get down and their horses are led to the horse lines and tied there, and they mount again once the camp is struck. Once your camp is up your army sleeps till dawn, the clock at its fastest; dawn strikes the camp and the clock drops to 1x. Y on the map does it all at once. F by a fire lights it or puts it out. A fire burns 5 kg of wood an hour, or none within 300 m of woods. At night a lit fire is seen 8 km off.

Rest stop Sleeps under its roofs
Inn 12
Guesthall 20
Coaching inn 30
Village (quartered) 4 a house or barn
Town 1 for every 8 citizens, at least 20
Castle 40
Seat 100

The rest camp beside it. Quartering on your own realm’s village eats its grain and costs its goodwill. Quartering on a hostile village is a raid. A camp kept on the same ground three days falls sick: 2% of the men outside fall badly wounded each dawn.

Camp life

Section titled “Camp life”

With the camp up the men live in it, yours and the AI’s alike. There are no camp followers: the soldiers do everything themselves.

  • Cooking. One man to each lit fire keeps a pot going, taking it in turns: grain becomes pottage, and fresh meat a stew or a roast. There are no mealtimes in camp. Each man goes to the fire to eat when he is hungry and goes back to what he was doing. With no fire lit, the men eat cold where they sit, and grain is no use.
  • The evening, dusk to 22:00. Each man does what his army’s mood says: talks round the fire, plays dice, smokes a pipe or sings, mends his kit, tends the horses, prays, sits quiet or sleeps early. Rain sends most of them to bed. The mood comes from the last day’s fight, a hot meal, wounds, the weather and whether their pay is owed.
  • The wounded lie by a fire and the best healer goes round them.
  • Rest days. When the camp stays up past a dawn, the men wash their linen and hang it on ropes by the wagons, fetch firewood from the woods into the train and water from a stream, and wrestle.
  • The watch walks the sentry ring with torches at night.

From afar a camp shows its firelight and cook smoke, and close by you hear the fires, low talk and laughter, a tune of an evening and the watch calling in the night.

Wounds

Section titled “Wounds”

Not every man who goes down is dead. The last blow decides whether he dies or is lightly or badly wounded, and armour softens it a step. The lightly wounded fight on, and the badly wounded ride in the train or walk at the back. Wounds heal by the hour, faster at rest, with a healer and with bandages and herbs, and left untended they turn bad and bring fevers. All of it, bleeding too, is on Wounds.

Blood

Section titled “Blood”

Blood shows as much as the Blood setting allows (Options, Gameplay: off, some or full). A blow throws a spurt of droplets the way it threw the man, the part struck stains red and darkens as it dries, and a pool spreads under the dead. Blood darkens on the ground over a few minutes, shows darker on snow and fainter on grass or mud. It lands on decks, floors and cave floors too. The weather washes it out slowly, never under a roof, and standing water 0.3 m deep washes it off.

After the fight

Section titled “After the fight”

The winner takes half the beaten army’s coin when it routs and the rest if it is wiped out, plus 8 silver for every man killed. Its train is left standing where it was beaten: all of a wiped-out army’s, and a routed one’s wagons, gear, goods and the food its mules cannot carry. Horses killed lie dead where they fell. A side holds the field after a quiet minute with no fighting enemy near. Riding alone, with no men yet, you count as one man, so you hold the field where you cut your foes down. Only the holder gathers it, by the rules on the field screen (G as the rider), or G with men picked sends them. Burying your dead under mounds lifts morale. A field nobody holds is picked over, a third a day.

A gatherer takes everything off one body in a trip, up to the 25 kg he can carry, and comes back for the rest. With the field screen’s men may take an upgrade tick on (it is by default), he may first swap one piece into his own kit if it is better than what he wears in its slot, but never one that changes his role, so a spearman never takes up a bow. Turn the tick off to keep the best pieces for yourself.

By your own hand, look at it and press F:

  • A body: loot it on the Character screen.
  • A mule, spare horse or wagon of a train left on the field: it follows you to your train with its load, and joins it on arriving. You drive a wagon yourself.
  • A dead horse: butcher it. Stay by it for three game hours and its 150 kg of fresh meat goes into the train’s food, as far as there is room. The field screen’s butcher rule sends your men to do it.

Fresh meat goes off in three days, so it is eaten first. See Economy.

Men of a routed army caught on foot by the army they broke from yield, about a third of them, and stand where they gave up until gathered. The gather order (G) sends men to rope them, a minute a man, and to lead a left train over to yours at a walk: it joins your train only when it gets there, and until then it can be lost if the men leading it drop the ropes and run. Yielded men with no hostile army within 120 m run off. Prisoners march roped behind the train, eat its food and need a guard of one man to five, or half of those past the guard slip away each dawn. Captives held three days can be offered a place in the ranks.

Walls, breaches and fire are on Sieges.
