Heavens

Everything in the sky follows the game’s clock alone, so co-op players see the same sky.

The sun

Section titled “The sun”

The sun circles the disc once a day, 150 km out, and passes under it at night. Night is the disc’s own shadow. Over the year its circle rises and sinks:

Day Daylight
Midsummer About 16 hours
Midwinter About 8 hours

Every few hours the sun erupts. A day later the eruption reaches the disc and lights the aurora.

The moons

Section titled “The moons”
Moon Look Orbit Size seen from the middle
Aoede Small, silver 4 days Two thirds of the sun
Isonoe Large, amber 16 days The sun’s own

The moons are flat coins with their faces turned down to the land. They have no crescent: a face brightens and dims with the angle the sunlight strikes it. At the midnight that begins the 9th day of every season, both moons stand high and bright together. That is the festival, and the day of the season’s feast.

Eclipses

Section titled “Eclipses”

When a moon passes before the sun, its shadow sweeps across the land. Aoede is too small to hide the sun and only dims it. Once a year, at noon on the spring festival, Isonoe hides the sun whole. Its shadow crosses the land in under a minute, and a place under its path stays nearly dark for about 10 seconds.

Night

Section titled “Night”

Night cuts how far armies see to 1.5 km, or 2.5 km under a full moon. Each full moon lights the land in its own colour.

Sight How often
Shooting star Every 1 to 3 hours; one in eight is a fireball, seen at any hour
Comet Every 4 years (256 days), for 12 to 20 days; the first on day 30
Meteor shower Night of the 11th of autumn, every 4 years
Dust ring shower First night of winter, every year
Aurora Nights after the sun erupts, stronger in winter
Nova About every 2 years, gone in 2 days
Supernova About every 8 years, fading over 4 days
Magnetar flare Once or twice a year
Sky waves 8 to 10 nights a year, fading over 10 hours

Four pulsars blink among the stars, each at its own beat. A ring of pale dust circles the disc 160 km out, just past the sun.

Tides

Section titled “Tides”

The sea rises and falls with pulls from the sun (0.4 m), Aoede (0.6 m) and Isonoe (0.9 m). Each body lifts the sea while it is up and lets it sink while it is under the disc. The sea never moves more than 1.9 m from its level, and the festival’s spring tides run highest. Lakes and rivers have no tide.

The air

Section titled “The air”

The sky is a dome of air the disc’s own size, standing on its rim and 20 km high. Outside it there is only the dark of space.
