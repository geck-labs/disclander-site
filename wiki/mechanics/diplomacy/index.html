Diplomacy

Houses

Section titled “Houses”

Five great houses share the continent: Red, Blue, Green, Gold and Purple. The emperor holds the throne town at the middle. Four free cities hold themselves. Ten other free captains, the Disclanders, ride the map from the start.

A town or castle taken in war goes to the lord whose army took it, or to the house’s ruler when no lord leads it. A town keeps its mayor, who turns with it.

The feudal chain

Section titled “The feudal chain”
Head Holds Owes
Emperor The throne town
Lord The house and its seat The emperor
Castellan A castle The lord
Knight A manor and its villages The castle
Mayor A town Its castle, or the seat for a capital
Reeve A village Its manor, or its castle

Every head is a person with a name, a face, the skills of the office and a record. Half are women, titled Lady. Knights lead retinues and their manor’s levies, castellans lead border raids, and lords lead their hosts.

When a head’s army is wiped out, he is killed (10%), taken (55%) or gets away. When it routs he mostly rides on, now and then wounded, taken (10%) or killed (3%). A captive is ransomed after 10 days for what his seat can pay:

Head Ransom
Reeve 50
Knight 150
Mayor 200
Castellan 300
Lord 1,000

A dead head’s seat stands empty 3 days, then an heir takes it.

Free and bound

Section titled “Free and bound”

A village drifts toward how bound it should be by its house’s men within a day of it. Below 0.2 it is free and sends no one when the banners are called. A town drifts between free and bound to the castle that claims it, a tenth of the way each day: its house’s men within 10 km against the town’s militia and wealth. A free town keeps its taxes.

Calling the banners

Section titled “Calling the banners”

The great houses fight with unpaid levies. Each house calls its banners at the start and again 8 days after its host breaks up. The lord’s host forms at his castle with a guard of 30 spearmen, 15 archers and 15 riders. Every bound village sends its share of men up the chain, and bound towns their militia’s share. The host waits up to three days for its musters, then goes on campaign. After 16 days of service, or once its army is gone, the host breaks up and its bands go home.

If you lead a house that holds a castle, you can call the banners from the realm screen.

Your oath

Section titled “Your oath”

You start as a free lord, a house of your own, at peace with every house. You can trade and hire in their towns. Falling on one of a house’s parties or armies puts you at war with that house for good.

To swear, ride to a lord’s seat while he is at home, open the Heads tab and press Swear oath (or O). You join his house and its wars, your towns and armies go with you, and a town your army takes becomes your fief. To renounce, go back to his seat and press Renounce oath (or L). Your fiefs go back to the lord. Your own loyalty never changes.

Loyalty

Section titled “Loyalty”

Each day a castellan or sworn lord with a fief grows more loyal (0.02), and one without grows restless (0.03). Losing a fief in war costs 0.1. The Temple’s blessing adds 0.01 a day and its condemnation takes 0.015. Below 0.2 he defects to the most powerful other house, taking his fiefs, everyone below him in the chain, and the armies he leads. Knights, reeves and mayors never drift.

The emperor

Section titled “The emperor”

The emperor’s authority fades 0.002 a day, and the lords’ loyalty to him with it. After 375 days it falls below a quarter, and every house’s lord breaks away and owes no one.

Coalitions

Section titled “Coalitions”

A house’s power is its soldiers plus 100 for every town it holds. When the strongest house passes 45% of everyone’s power, and at least two others have any, the others ally against it and stop fighting each other until it falls below 35%.

Disclanders

Section titled “Disclanders”

Each Disclander starts as you do, alone with 100 gold, each by a hideout of its own, and has a temper from cautious to bold. They hire, trade, hunt bandits, take jobs and claim bounties. A Disclander with men hunts your armies when it outnumbers you enough: 1.8 to one for the most cautious, down to 1.2 for the boldest. Bold ones rob peaceful parties. At 120 men a cautious one swears to the nearest house at peace, and a bold one marches on its enemies. Beaten, it raises a new band of 20 at home three days later.

News

Section titled “News”

What happens is known first near where it happened, and travels only with people on the roads. Your house hears what is known where your armies stand, at your own towns, and from parties you talk to. What happens within 3 km of one of your armies is seen at once. Other houses are known only as last heard of. See Travellers.
