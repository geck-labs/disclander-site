Band

Who sits in each seat of this tune's band. Pick another instrument for a seat, or nobody, and set how loud that seat is. It changes on the next bar. Every tune keeps its own band.

Inside the instruments

Fine settings of how five of the instruments are built. They are small changes, and they are the same for every tune that uses the instrument. Each line says what to listen for.

Settings to send back

One band plays without stopping, writing the music a bar at a time. There are no songs: it never begins again and never ends. Pick another tune and the band turns on the next bar, in the middle of things, its speed sliding over the bars after. There is no slow and fast style of a tune any more: a fast tune is a tune of its own, and Chase is the first. Into Chase the beat drops in: the band thins out over the lead-in, falls silent for a beat, and everything lands at once; while it stays on Chase it breaks down and drops again now and then, every other time with the bass landing a bar and a half late. In the fast tunes (Chase, Battle, Siege) everything but the drums dips a little as each kick lands (the Pumping knob under Mix; 0 turns it off). Chase is built on one track: a steady 116 beats a minute, the kick drum on beats one and three and once more just before the bar ends, the snare on two and four, a heavy growling bass on one low note that steps up for the middle of each bar and back. Its parts go twice as quick as that track's (hats and tune in sixteenth notes, the bass struck twice a beat), and its band is a rock band: two overdriven guitars, one to each side, chugging the bass's note low and short, a third with the tune, and no soft pad. The kick and snare are loud, with a low thump under each main kick and a run of quick kicks closing every fourth bar, and the bass hits hardest on beats one and three. Chase also moves between four parts, four or eight bars each, picked as it goes and named in the line under the buttons: its main part; the charge (a kick every half beat, guitars and bass twice as quick, the tune higher); half speed (one kick and one snare a bar, long ringing guitar notes); and the stops (guitars, bass and kick strike together six times a bar with gaps between, and the tune rests). Battle and Siege are built the same way as Chase, with the same four parts, and differ in what you can hear at once: Battle is the quickest and hardest (160 beats a minute), its kick drum gallops along with the guitars on every beat, and its tune is on a war horn (brass blown hard, each note bursting in dull and opening to a blare); Siege (132) has deep war drums in place of most of the kit, a choir held underneath, and its tune on bowed strings or sung. The upright lines in the picture are the bars. When something sounds right, copy the settings and send them over, and they become the game's own. The settings text lists every band and instrument you have changed, for all the tunes.
